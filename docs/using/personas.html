Working with Omnissiah

Personas

A persona is a name plus a system prompt: a description of how the assistant behaves in this conversation. Switch one on and it replaces the standard instructions of Omnissiah; it does not come on top of them.

They live behind Prompts in the sidebar, together with your saved prompts. That button takes you to the page Prompts, with three tabs: Prompts, Personas and Persona groups. The tab is in the address, so /prompts?tab=personas opens this one right away.

Making one

On the Personas tab you click New persona. You fill in two fields:

  • Name, for example "Critical editor".
  • System prompt: "Describe how this persona behaves…"

On the page every row starts a New chat with that persona on it. In the menu next to it are Edit and Delete.

Switching one on in a running conversation

Click the + on the input bar and choose Insert prompt. That opens a window with the same three sections in a list on the left - deliberately no navigation, because you are in the middle of a conversation. Go to Personas there. What you get is a compact version: pick an existing persona from the dropdown or make one with New, and click Start persona. If you change the text of an existing persona, that change is saved as soon as you start it.

An active persona appears as a chip above the input bar, with a cross to switch it off again.

Who wins in a conflict

If you work in a project, your project instructions and your persona are both in the prompt. The order is: project first, then the conversation. The more specific layer comes later and therefore wins. See Projects.

Persona groups

A group is a list of personas you put on a conversation in one go. On the Persona groups tab you make one with New group: a name, and the members underneath. You add members with Add persona…, drag them by the handle to order them (the keyboard works too) and take them out again with the cross. A group without members cannot be saved.

You pick the mode when starting, not when making it. Next to the start button is an arrow with two choices:

  • Joint answer - "The personas give one answer together." The system prompts of the members are merged, in the order you set, into one instruction: combine those perspectives and speak with one voice. So you get one answer, just as usual.
  • Separate replies - "Every persona replies on its own." Your question gets several answers underneath each other, each as its own card with the name of the persona above it and its own stop button while it writes.

With separate replies every persona runs its own thread: it sees all your messages and its own earlier answers, but not those of the others. So they do not influence each other, and that is the point of the setting.

The main button always starts in Joint answer; the arrow is there for the other choice.

Deleting

A persona can always be deleted, even when it is still switched on somewhere. The confirmation says what happens then: "Active conversations with this persona fall back to the default system prompt." With a group the same applies, and it says that the personas themselves stay.

A persona is an instruction, not a source

That difference counts in a project with Answer only from the sources. What you write in a persona is simply in the prompt, but it carries no source number and can therefore not be cited as a source. The promise is about where the answer comes from, not about how the assistant writes. See Projects.
