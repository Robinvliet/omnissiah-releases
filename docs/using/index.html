Working with Omnissiah

This part grows along with the app. The first four pages describe what you work in every day: a conversation and what it can do, projects to group work that belongs together, the sources the model can see, and what a linked folder adds to that.

Then comes what you put around it: the models you talk to, tools from outside, work that runs on a schedule, and the three ways to bend the assistant to your hand - a role, reusable instructions, and what it remembers about you. The last page lists the keyboard shortcuts.

  • ConversationsWhat a conversation can do, steering an answer, and what happens when it gets long.
  • ProjectsGrouping conversations around one subject, with shared instructions and shared sources.
  • SourcesDocuments, folders and attachments in one list, and what the model sees of them each turn.
  • Working in a folderLetting the assistant read and change files on your own machine, with approval up front and safety nets underneath.
  • ModelsCloud models with your own key, local models through Ollama or LM Studio, your own endpoints, and how you choose between them.
  • MCP serversHanging extra tools on the assistant through the Model Context Protocol, and deciding which of them join.
  • Scheduled tasksWork that runs on a schedule while you are not there, in a task conversation of its own.
  • PersonasGiving the assistant a fixed role and tone, alone or in a group that answers together or separately.
  • SkillsReusable instructions you switch on per conversation, written yourself or fetched from elsewhere.
  • MemoryWhat the assistant remembers about you between conversations, where it lives and how you clear it.
  • ShortcutsThe defaults per platform, and how to make them your own.
