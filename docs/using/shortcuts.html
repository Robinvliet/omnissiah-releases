Working with Omnissiah

Shortcuts

Sixteen actions in Omnissiah have a keyboard shortcut. You will find all of them under Settings → Shortcuts, in the same order as below.

The defaults

These are the combinations Omnissiah starts with. Read them as a starting point and not as a law: they differ per platform (the columns below), and you can change every one of them, see the next section. If you changed something, your version is the correct one and this table is not; the overview in Settings always shows what applies on this device.

This table is pulled from the app itself on every build, so it does not lag behind what the app does.

Choosing your own combination

Go to Settings → Shortcuts and click the keys of the row you want to change. The row starts recording: "Press a key combination…", and whatever you press appears right away, a lone Shift included. Enter saves, Esc cancels, and clicking outside the row cancels too.

Two things block saving, both with the reason underneath instead of a silent refusal:

  • A combination without a real modifier. "Add ⌘ or ⌥ - a bare key would hijack typing." Escape and the F-keys are the exception, because they do not type anything themselves.
  • A combination that is already taken. "Already used by …", with the action named. Nothing is ever overwritten quietly.

A row you changed gets a ↺ to put just that one back. At the top there is Reset all for the whole list, behind a confirmation.

Shortcuts apply per device. They live in the browser or the desktop app where you set them, not in your database, so they do not travel to your phone or your other computer. Only your deviations are stored: whatever you leave alone follows the default by itself, even when that default changes later.

Where a shortcut deliberately does not fire

Three situations in which nothing happens, and every time that is the intention:

  • While you are typing. With your cursor in a text field, combinations that could produce a character do nothing. That matters most on a keyboard with AltGr: AltGr is Ctrl+Alt, so AltGr+P types a character and must not suddenly pin a conversation. Combinations with ⌘, and keys that type nothing themselves (Escape, Enter, the arrows, the F-keys), do work everywhere.
  • When something else already handles the key. If a dialog is open that uses Escape to close itself, it wins over "stop generating".
  • While you are recording a new combination. Every keystroke is input for that combination then, and nothing else.

Holding a key down does not repeat a shortcut: only the first press counts.
